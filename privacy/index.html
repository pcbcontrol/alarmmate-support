1. Who is responsible and what this policy covers

Francesc Roig is responsible for AlarmMate and this website. For privacy requests and support, use this email link: Privacy requests.

This policy covers AlarmMate Sensors (AlarmMate) for iOS, its communication with compatible sensor modules, and this public privacy and support website. Optional services are described below so that you can decide which to enable.

AlarmMate has no developer-operated account system or central database of your sensor history. The app does not contain advertising SDKs or cross-app advertising tracking. Optional network services, website hosting, and support correspondence can involve the processing described in this policy.

2. Information used on your device

AlarmMate uses the following information to connect to your sensors, apply your settings, and show events:

  • Sensors and settings: paired sensor identifiers and names, connection details, alarm and detector settings, schedules, language, and display preferences.
  • Sensor readings and history: motion and noise events, numerical sound levels, battery and power information, event timestamps, notification text, and read/unread status. The app does not record audio; sound detection uses numerical readings from the module's microphone.
  • Credentials: pairing tokens and, when you configure them, Wi-Fi names and passwords, eduroam profiles/certificates, SSH credentials, and information needed for optional connections. Sensitive credentials use the iOS Keychain or the protection supplied by the relevant service. Configuration needed by a paired module is also sent to that module.
  • Simulated examples: fictitious sensors, settings, and events used to explore the app. They are identified as simulated.

This app information is stored on the device. History is not automatically sent to the developer. You can export history as a CSV file and share it with recipients you choose.

3. Information sent to modules and external services

External processing depends on the features you use:

Why Tailscale data is needed: account, node and connection information supports sign-in, device authorization and coordination of the optional remote sensor connection. AlarmMate does not use this information for advertising or its own usage analytics. Tailscale documents the identity-provider information it receives, including name, email and sometimes a profile-photo URL, in its SSO data-access documentation. Its identity documentation explains how user and node identities determine network access. Other optional integrations, such as institutional Wi-Fi sign-in, have the purposes described separately below.

  • Paired modules: receive commands, configuration, required network credentials, and notification-recipient information. Modules keep their own settings and may communicate with the networks and services you configure.
  • Apple notifications and Live Activities: when configured, the module or notification provider sends Apple the installation's delivery token and notification or activity content. This can include the sensor name/identifier, event type and time, numerical noise level, and alert text. Apple processes delivery information and may temporarily retain pending messages. Notifications can appear on a locked device according to your iOS settings. See Apple’s privacy policy.
  • Tailscale, optional remote access: The distributed AlarmMate app does not include Tailscale client log uploads, their local log buffers or associated client-metric telemetry. Remote diagnostic telemetry handlers are also excluded. The service still processes your account name and email address, persistent user and node identifiers, device names, operating system, IP addresses, public keys and technical connection information, including node status and last-seen information, to authenticate the node and operate the connections you request. Tailscale states that traffic within its network is end-to-end encrypted. These exclusions apply to the node embedded in AlarmMate; other devices in your network have their own logging settings. Earlier app builds may continue to upload client logs until updated. This does not delete account or node records held by Tailscale, or logs sent by earlier app versions. Previously uploaded client logs remain subject to the provider's retention policy. See Tailscale’s privacy policy and retention policy.
  • eduroam setup, optional: the app requests the public profile directory and the configuration/authentication services of your selected institution. Your email domain is matched to the discovery directory locally. When you start sign-in, your full email address is sent to the selected institution’s authorization service to help identify your account. Those services also receive connection metadata and the information required for your chosen sign-in and profile. Consult your institution's notice and the eduroam privacy notice.
  • Firmware downloads: checking or downloading compatible firmware makes requests to GitHub. GitHub receives normal request metadata, such as your IP address, and authentication information when needed for the download. See GitHub’s privacy statement.
  • Services you configure: MQTT brokers, SSH hosts, network administrators, and other selected infrastructure process the information needed for those services. Their access and retention depend on your configuration and provider.

We do not sell app data or share it for advertising. Sharing is limited to providing the functions you request, support you initiate, or legal obligations. Any provider processing personal data on our behalf must protect it consistently with this policy and applicable data-protection law. Services you activate under your own account also apply their published privacy terms.

4. Permissions and your choices

Bluetooth is used to discover and communicate with compatible modules. Local-network access is used for direct connections on your network. Notifications are used to show alerts, and Live Activities can display current information on the Lock Screen or Dynamic Island when enabled.

You can change permissions in iOS Settings, change the alarm and per-sensor notification controls, and disable optional integrations. Refusing notifications does not prevent access to the app's other screens. The app does not request access to your phone's microphone, contacts, photos, or GPS location.

Disabling an integration stops the app from using it; it does not by itself delete an external account or previously stored records.

5. Retention, deletion, and backups

  • Settings and credentials: remain while stored by the app or the relevant service. Removing a sensor clears its local registration and sensor-specific credentials, but reusable saved networks and the separate Tailscale state may remain.
  • History: is kept locally with a bounded number of entries; older entries are replaced as the limit is reached. Use the history screen's clear action to delete it. Removing a sensor does not clear the separate history. Exported CSV copies remain under the control of the recipient and storage destination you select.
  • Module and service data: use the module's reset/reassignment procedures and the external service's deletion controls when needed. Turning off Tailscale or removing a sensor does not delete your Tailscale account or all module settings.
  • iOS storage and backups: system backups may include some app data depending on your device settings. Some Keychain credentials can persist after uninstalling the app. Uninstalling alone should not be treated as proof that all credentials, backups, module data, or external-service data have been erased.
  • Support correspondence: if you contact us, we receive your email address, message, and attachments. We use them to answer and investigate your request, and keep them only while needed for the request, related follow-up, or a legal obligation. You may request deletion of support copies.

We cannot remotely read or erase records that exist only on your device or module. Contact us if you need help locating the available deletion controls, including for stored credentials.

6. Security and protection

AlarmMate uses platform-protected credential storage, authenticated module connections, and encrypted transports where supported by the selected feature. Security also depends on your device, firmware, network, broker, and service settings. No storage or communication method provides an absolute guarantee.

Choose sensor names that are suitable for notification previews. Do not send passwords, private keys, access tokens, or unnecessary personal information when requesting support.

7. Purposes and legal grounds

Information is used to provide the sensor connections, configuration, alerts, and optional services you request. Where applicable, the legal grounds are providing the requested service, consent for processing that requires it, legitimate interests in answering support requests and maintaining security, and compliance with legal obligations.

Where processing relies on consent, you can withdraw it by disabling the relevant permission or feature, or by contacting us for information held through support. Withdrawal does not affect processing already lawfully carried out. AlarmMate does not use app data to make automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effects.

8. Your privacy rights

Depending on the law applicable to you, you may request access, correction, erasure, restriction, or portability of personal data, or object to processing. Use the Privacy requests email link and describe your request. Please do not include account secrets. We may ask for information needed to verify and process the request.

For records held by an independent service, use that service's privacy controls or contact it directly. Providers may process data in other countries under their published terms and applicable safeguards. You can also complain to your competent data-protection authority. See the European Commission’s explanation of privacy rights.

9. Privacy of this website

This static website is hosted by GitHub Pages. We have not added analytics, advertising, third-party embeds, forms, or our own cookies. GitHub logs and stores visitors' IP addresses for security purposes, including for visitors who are not signed in. See GitHub Pages data collection and GitHub’s privacy statement.

Email links open your email app; sending an email shares your address, message, and any attachments with us and the email providers involved. External links are governed by the destination's policies.

10. Changes and contact

We update this policy when relevant app functions or data practices change. The date at the top identifies the current version. Material changes will be explained in the updated policy and, where appropriate, in the app or release information.
